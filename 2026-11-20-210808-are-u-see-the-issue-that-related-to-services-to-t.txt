● I've read through the project. Nothing has been changed. Here's what I found
  that matters for the services feature.

  How booking works today

  Booking is built around the doctor. The path is clinic → (specialty) → doctor
  → availability rule → slot or queue spot → appointment. "What the patient is
  coming for" is never recorded as data.

  - Prices are two fixed fields on Doctor: examinationFee (فشكلا رعس) and
    consultationFee (ةراشتسالا رعس). Nothing is priced per clinic or per branch.
  - Duration comes from the doctor's AvailabilityRule:
    - Slot mode: slotDurationMin cuts the day into equal pre-generated Slot rows
      (generateSlotsForRule).
    - Order and arrival modes: estimatedDurationMin drives the expected time and
      wait estimate. A snapshot is copied onto the queue and the appointment.
  - Appointment doesn't record a type or service. The only "why" is the
    free-text patientNotes.
  - One open booking per patient per doctor. Both createAppointment and
    bookOrderAppointment enforce this.
  - Four places create bookings, and all of them would need a service choice:
    - the AI agent: book_appointment / book_order_appointment in
      agent/tools/patient.ts
    - the patient web modal: components/landing/book-appointment-modal.tsx (step
      1 picks day and slot, step 2 adds notes and confirms)
    - admin/staff booking for a patient: server/actions/admin.ts:431
    - doctor referrals: referAppointment
  - Medical records already list what was done. TreatmentProcedure has kind, a
    free-text name and an optional cost. It's filled in after the visit and
    isn't connected to booking.

  Problems I found

  1. No service catalog. A clinic offering cleaning, laser, X-ray, fillings and
     so on can't list them, price them, or let patients book them.
  2. The examination and consultation prices are used inconsistently:
     - The agent's booking card and appointment-detail.tsx show examinationFee.
     - The landing doctors list, the web booking modal and the admin "Book
       appointment" button show consultationFee.
     - The solo-doctor landing page shows examinationFee ?? consultationFee.
     - A doctor can edit only consultationFee on their own profile page.

     So the patient can see a different price depending on where they book.
  3. The agent assumes every visit is a فشك. The prompt asks «؟هيإ صوصخب فشكلا»
     and stores the answer as notes. The prompt even suggests clinics put lists
     like "تاصوحفلا ءامسأ" into knowledge docs, which is a workaround for this
     same gap.
  4. Durations are fixed per rule. A 60-minute procedure would take one
     30-minute slot, and queue time estimates assume every patient takes the
     same time.
  5. The one-open-booking-per-doctor rule would block a patient from booking an
     examination plus a separate procedure with the same doctor.

  Questions to settle before building

  1. Who provides a service? Always a doctor, or can some services have no
     doctor (lab, X-ray, nursing, a device room)? This is the biggest design
     decision, because the whole scheduling system currently assumes a doctor.
  2. Price level: per clinic, per doctor, or per clinic with an optional
     per-doctor override? Per branch too?
  3. Duration: should a service's duration actually change scheduling (for
     example, a 60-minute service takes two consecutive slots), or is it only
     shown to the patient?
  4. Examination and consultation: do they become ordinary services that are
     created automatically for each doctor, or stay as special fields alongside
     a new service list?
  5. Is choosing a service required when booking, or optional with examination
     as the default?
  6. Should a booked service link to the TreatmentProcedure list after the
     visit, and later to billing or revenue reports? There's no billing yet.
  7. Agent flow: should the patient name a service first ("I want teeth
     cleaning") and the agent find doctors who offer it, or pick a doctor first
     and then a service?

  Tell me how you see it, and we can go through these one at a time.

  Design decisions behind it

  1. Flat services, no separate variants table. "رزيل – Face" and "رزيل – Full
     legs" are two services in the "رزيل" category. That's one concept fewer,
     and the agent can still search and group by category. If clinics end up
     with large matrices (area × device × branch), a ServiceVariant table could
     come later. I'd leave it out of v1.
  2. kind is a coarse type, separate from the clinic's own category. It lets the
     agent behave correctly ("book a consultation first", "a follow-up is
     cheaper"), and it can feed reports and the existing ProcedureKind in
     medical records.
  3. "Assign services to doctors based on his schedule" works at two levels:
     - DoctorService records what the doctor can do.
     - RuleService optionally limits a specific schedule (day or branch) to some
       services, for example laser days vs. injection days.

     When a patient picks a service, availability only shows days and slots
     whose rule allows that service.
  4. Appointments get line items instead of a single serviceId. Aesthetic
     patients often combine services in one visit (laser Face + Under arms), and
     dental visits often do too. The price and duration are copied at booking
     time, so later price changes don't rewrite history.
  5. Examination and consultation fees become services. A migration creates a
      service for each doctor )FOLLOW_UP( "ةراشتسا" and a )CONSULTATION( "فشك"
     from the existing fees. Then examinationFee and consultationFee are
     removed, which also fixes the price inconsistency I found earlier.
  6. Clinic-type templates: when a clinic is set up (or from the services page),
     it can pick "Dental / Aesthetic & Derma / Nutrition / General / Physio" to
     pre-fill typical categories and services with no prices. After that it's
     ordinary editable data.

  What I'd leave out of v1

  - Prepaid packages with balances (pulses or sessions bought upfront and
    deducted per visit, with expiry). This is really a patient wallet / billing
    feature. In v1 a package can exist as a kind = PACKAGE service, so the agent
    can quote and describe it, but nothing tracks the balance.
  - Equipment as a schedulable resource (Cynosure vs Deka). For v1, include the
    device in the service name or branch price.
  - A full promotions engine. compareAtPrice + offerLabel + offerEndsAt covers
    everything in this PDF.

  Questions for you

  1. Who performs the service? At InShape, laser is often done by a technician
     or nurse, not a doctor. Should the "doctor" in our system just mean
     "practitioner" (any bookable provider), or do services need to be bookable
     without a doctor at all?
  2. Should duration drive scheduling? For example, laser Full body = 60 min
     takes 2 consecutive 30-minute slots. Or do we only show the duration and
     keep slots as they are? The first is more correct but touches slot booking.
  3. Line items or a single service? Can one appointment hold several services,
     or is one service per booking enough for now?
  4. Which booking flow should the agent use? "Service first → doctors who do it
     → availability", "doctor first → their services", or both? I'd suggest
     both, with service-first as the default for aesthetic clinics.
  5. Does the one-open-booking-per-doctor rule stay? With services, a patient
     may need an examination and a separate procedure with the same doctor.
  6. Packages: are you OK leaving the balance tracking to a later phase?

  Answer as many as you like and I'll update the design. Nothing gets built
  until we've agreed on it.
